## Concept 1.1 – What is a Group?

### A. Explanation

**Definition (Schein, 1980):**
> "Any number of people who interact with each other and perceive themselves to be a group."

A **group** is a collection of individuals who come together because of a shared purpose, task, or identity. Simply being in the same place doesn't make a group — the members must *interact* and *identify* as a unit.

**Gordon (2001) added four characteristics of a group:**

| Characteristic | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Members see themselves as a UNIT** | Group identity exists in the minds of members |
| **Group provides REWARDS for all** | Being in the group benefits each member |
| **CORRESPONDING effects** | Members influence and are influenced by each other |
| **Common GOALS** | They share a purpose or objective |

**Key idea:** A group is not just a crowd or a collection. It is a social unit with shared identity, mutual influence, and common goals.

## Concept 1.3 – Groups vs. Teams

### A. Explanation

Though often used interchangeably, **Groups** and **Teams** have important differences.

**Definition of a Work Team (Sundstrom, DeMuse & Futrell, 1990):**
> "A team is an interdependent collection of individuals who work together toward a common goal and who share responsibility for specific outcomes for their organizations."

| Comparison Point | GROUP | TEAM |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Generic, broad | Mostly in organizational settings |
| Dependency | Members may or may not depend on each other | Members are **interdependent** |
| Collaboration | Members work together or share resources | Members work on tasks that are **interdependent** |
| Common usage | Both terms used interchangeably in practice | Same |

**Key difference:** Teams require **interdependence** — each member's work directly affects others. Groups can be more loosely connected.

**Work Team Characteristics (Devine et al., 1999):**
> "A collection of 3 or more individuals who interact intensively to provide an organizational product, plan, decision or service."

**5 Key Dynamics of a Work Team:**
1. **Identification** – Members identify with the team, not just the broader group
2. **Interdependence** – Relying on each other's expertise and assistance
3. **Power Differentiation** – Respecting roles; asking indirect questions, being polite
4. **Social Distance** – Reduced by being casual, using nicknames, showing empathy
5. **Conflict Management** – Collaboration, compromising, win-win negotiation

## Concept 1.4 – Factors Affecting Group Performance

### A. Explanation

Several internal and external factors determine how well a group performs. These are:

#### 1. Group Cohesiveness
*"About liking and trusting"*

Cohesiveness = degree to which group members are attracted to each other and motivated to stay.

**Factors increasing cohesiveness:**
- Group Status
- Group Size (smaller = more cohesive)
- Outside Pressure
- Isolation
- Stability of Membership
- Homogeneity (similar members)

**Effects of high cohesiveness:**
- Higher productivity and efficiency
- Better decision quality
- Higher member satisfaction
- More member interaction
- Greater employee courtesy

#### 2. Group Ability & Confidence
Groups with more capable and confident members perform better.

#### 3. Personality of Members
Individual personalities directly affect group dynamics.

#### 4. Communication Structure
How information flows within the group.

#### 5. Group Roles
- **Task-oriented roles** – focused on completing the work
- **Social-oriented roles** – focused on maintaining relationships

#### 6. Presence of Others (Social Facilitation/Inhibition)

| Phenomenon | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Audience Effect** | Performance changes when watched by others |
| **Coaction** | Performing alongside others doing the same task |
| **Social Inhibition** | Performance decreases due to presence of others |
| **Social Loafing** | Members reduce effort when in a group |
| **Free Rider** | Benefiting from group without contributing |
| **Sucker Effect** | Hardworking members reduce effort to avoid being exploited |

#### 7. Individual Dominance
One member dominating discussions can suppress others.

#### 8. Groupthink
Occurs when group members suppress dissent to maintain harmony — leading to poor decisions.

**Groupthink occurs when group:**
- Is cohesive
- Is insulated from qualified outsiders
- Has an illusion of invulnerability and/or infallibility
- Believes it is morally superior to adversaries
- Is under great pressure to conform
- Has a leader who promotes a favourite solution
- Has **gatekeepers** who keep information from other members

**Reducing Groupthink – The leader should:**
- State position strongly
- Promote open discussion
- Create subgroups to invite disagreements

## Concept 1.6 – Team/Group Formation: Tuckman's Model

### A. Explanation

**Tuckman & Jensen (1977)** proposed a 5-stage model of group development:

| Stage | Name | What Happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Forming** | Group comes together; members are polite, uncertain; getting to know each other |
| 2 | **Storming** | Conflict and disagreements arise; members compete for roles |
| 3 | **Norming** | Group establishes norms, roles, and cohesion develops |
| 4 | **Performing** | Group works effectively toward the goal; high productivity |
| 5 | **Adjourning** | Group dissolves after completing the task |

**Key insight:** Every team goes through these stages. Conflict (Storming) is *normal* and necessary before the team becomes productive (Performing).

## Concept 1.7 – Why Teams Don't Always Work

### A. Explanation

Despite their benefits, teams often fail due to these common reasons:

| Reason | Details |
|---|---|
| **Excessive Meeting Requirements** | Too many/long meetings waste time; discussions go off-topic |
| **Lack of Empowerment** | No authority given; managers fear quality loss; members reject empowerment |

## Concept 1.8 – Group Membership & Behaviour Theories

### A. Explanation

Two important theories explain why people behave the way they do in groups:

#### Social Identity Theory (Tajfel & Turner, 1979)
- People derive part of their **self-concept** from the groups they belong to.
- We categorize ourselves and others into groups ("us" vs. "them").
- This leads to **in-group favoritism** (supporting our own group) and sometimes **out-group bias**.
- Example: Engineers identifying strongly as "COEP students" and feeling pride in that identity.

#### Social Exchange Theory (Homans, 1961)
- People join and remain in groups based on a **cost-benefit analysis**.
- If the **rewards** of group membership outweigh the **costs**, people stay.
- If costs exceed rewards, people leave the group.
- Example: Staying in a study group because the help received outweighs the time spent.

## Concept 1.10 – Group Conflict

### A. Explanation

**Definition of Conflict:**
> A psychological and behavioural reaction to a perception that another person is:
> - Keeping you from reaching a goal
> - Taking away your right to behave in a particular way
> - Violating the expectancies of a relationship

Conflict is **perception-based** — it begins with how someone *feels* about another's actions.

---

#### Types of Conflict

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Individual vs. Individual | Personal conflict between two people |
| Individual vs. Team | One person in conflict with the whole group |
| Team vs. Team | Two groups in conflict |

---

#### Causes of Conflict

1. **Competition for Resources** – More demand, less supply
2. **Task Interdependence** – One's work depends on another; delays create friction
3. **Jurisdictional Ambiguity** – Unclear boundaries of authority/responsibility
4. **Communication Barriers:**
   - Physical (distance, noise)
   - Cultural (different norms)
   - Psychological (personal biases)
5. **Beliefs:**
   - Feeling superior to others
   - Feeling mistreated
   - Feeling vulnerable
   - Cannot trust others
   - Feeling helpless/powerless
6. **Personality** – Incompatible personalities cause friction

---

#### Difficult Personality Types (Brinkman & Kirschner, 2006)

| Type | Characteristic |
|---|---|
| **High need for Control** | Wants to dominate; "my way or highway" |
| **High need for Attention** | Always seeks spotlight; disrupts others |
| **High need for Approval** | Avoids conflict; people-pleaser |
| **High need for Perfection** | Overly critical; slows progress with excessive standards |

---

#### Conflict Styles/Strategies

| Style | Description |
|---|---|
| **Forcing / Competing** | Imposing your will; win-lose approach |
| **Negotiating / Bargaining** | Give and take; compromise |
| **Withdrawal / Avoidance** | Ignoring the conflict; walking away |
| **Triangling** | Involving a third party (not mediation; gossip-based) |

---

#### Resolving Conflict

**1. Formal Policy at Organizations** – Rules and procedures in place

**2. Conflict Resolution Skills:**
- Cooperation, compliments, avoiding negative interactions
- Finding mutual similarities, identifying common goals
- Reduce tension → Increase trust
- Planned meetings and dispute forums
- Cooperative Problem Solving

**3. Third-Party Intervention:**

| Method | Description |
|---|---|
| **Mediation** | Neutral third party helps reach a *mutual agreement* |
| **Arbitration** | Third party listens and *chooses the correct side* (binding decision) |

> **Key Difference:** Mediation = parties decide together. Arbitration = third party decides.

## Concept 2.4 – Leader & the Situation: Fiedler's Contingency Model

### A. Explanation

**Fiedler's Contingency Model (1960s):**
> Leadership effectiveness depends on the **interaction between the leader and the situation**. No single leadership style works in all situations.

**Key Tool: LPC Scale (Least Preferred Coworker)**
- Leaders rate the person they work with least effectively.
- **High LPC score** = relationship-oriented leader (rates even worst coworker positively)
- **Low LPC score** = task-oriented leader (rates worst coworker very negatively)

**Situation Favourability** depends on three factors:

| Factor | Description |
|---|---|
| **Task Structuredness** | Is the task clear and well-defined? |
| **Leader Position Power** | How much authority does the leader have? |
| **Leader-Member Relation** | How well does the leader get along with the group? |

**Finding:** Task-oriented leaders (low LPC) work best in either very favourable or very unfavourable situations. Relationship-oriented leaders (high LPC) work best in moderately favourable situations.

**Limitation:** "Not much research evidence" (as noted in slides).

## Concept 2.6 – Hersey & Blanchard's Situational Leadership

### A. Explanation

**Hersey & Blanchard's Situational Theory** says the best leadership style depends on the **readiness (ability + willingness)** of the subordinate.

**4 Subordinate Types and Matching Styles:**

| Subordinate Type | Characteristic | Recommended Leader Style |
|---|---|---|
| **S1** | Unable AND Unwilling (or insecure) | **Telling/Directing** – High task, low relationship |
| **S2** | Unable BUT Willing (or confident) | **Coaching/Selling** – High task, high relationship |
| **S3** | Able BUT Unwilling (or insecure) | **Supporting/Participating** – Low task, high relationship |
| **S4** | Able AND Willing (or confident) | **Delegating** – Low task, low relationship |

**Key insight:** As subordinates grow in ability and willingness, the leader's style should shift from directing → coaching → supporting → delegating.

## Concept 2.9 – Leadership through Decision Making: Vroom & Yetton Model

### A. Explanation

**Vroom & Yetton (1973) – Decision Making Model:**

**Key ideas:**
- Decisions by leaders are best only in specific situations.
- Mostly, decisions are better when made **with participation** by others (subordinates, colleagues, or both).
- Leadership performance can be improved by **training leaders in decision making**.

| **Consultative** | Leader consults others but makes the final decision alone |
| **Group** | Leader and group make the decision together (consensus) |

**Decision Tree:**
A structured diagram that guides leaders through a series of questions about the situation (quality requirement, subordinate acceptance, time constraints) to determine the best decision style.

## Concept 2.12 – Charismatic Leadership

### A. Explanation

**Charismatic Leadership** is a form of leadership based on the exceptional personal qualities and inspirational power of the leader.

**Key Characteristics of Charismatic Leaders:**

1. **Strong need for power** – Drive to influence and lead
2. **Supremely confident** – Believe deeply in themselves
3. **Engage in impressive behaviours** – Self-praise, demonstration of abilities
4. **Articulate an appealing vision** – Paint a compelling picture of the future

## C. Case Study Questions – Unit 1 Practice

### Case Study 1 (Group Dynamics)
*A team of 8 engineers is working on a critical project. After one month, they have established clear roles and are working efficiently. However, two senior members dominate all decisions and suppress others' suggestions. The rest of the group agrees even when they disagree.*

**Questions:**
(a) At what stage of Tuckman's model is this team?
(b) What two group problems are present?
(c) Suggest two remedies.

**Model Answer:**
(a) The team is at the **Performing stage** (working efficiently) but has underlying **Groupthink** issues.
(b) Problems: (1) **Individual Dominance** – seniors dominating, (2) **Groupthink** – others suppressing dissent.
(c) Remedies: (1) Leader should **promote open discussion** and invite disagreements; (2) **Create subgroups** to explore alternatives independently.

---

### Case Study 2 (Leadership Style)
*Manager Arvind works with a junior engineer Pooja who is highly skilled but often demotivated and reluctant to take initiative. Arvind sits down with her regularly, asks for her opinions on project decisions, and encourages her involvement.*

**Questions:**
(a) What type of subordinate is Pooja according to Hersey & Blanchard?
(b) What leadership style is Arvind using?
(c) Is it appropriate? Justify.

**Model Answer:**
(a) Pooja is an **S3 subordinate** – Able but Unwilling (or insecure).
(b) Arvind is using the **Supporting/Participating** style.
(c) Yes, it is appropriate. The Supporting style (low task, high relationship) is recommended for S3 subordinates. By involving Pooja in decisions and regularly engaging with her, Arvind addresses her motivational gap while respecting her existing competence.

---

### Case Study 3 (Conflict Resolution – from actual MSE paper)
*Two teams in a company disagree over resource allocation. The HR manager brings both teams together to discuss their concerns and helps them reach a mutually agreeable solution.*

**Question:** What type of third-party intervention is this? How is it different from arbitration?

**Model Answer:**
This is **Mediation** – a neutral third party (HR manager) facilitates discussion and helps the parties reach a **mutual agreement**. The parties themselves make the final decision.

**Arbitration** is different: the arbitrator listens to both sides and **chooses the correct side** – the decision is made by the third party, not the parties themselves.
