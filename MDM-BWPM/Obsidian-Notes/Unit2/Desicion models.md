# Bounded Rationality

This is the **second decision-making model** in your PDF, after the Rational Model. Your PDF emphasizes **limited information and limited time**, along with **satisficing, time pressure and shortcuts**.

## What is Bounded Rationality?

The idea is:

> We want to make a rational decision, **but our ability to do so is limited**.

In real life, we usually don't have:

- unlimited time,
    
- complete information,
    
- unlimited resources,
    
- the ability to evaluate every possible alternative.
    

So instead of finding the **perfect/best** solution, we often choose a solution that is **good enough**.

That is called **satisficing**.

---

## The key idea

### Rational Model

> **“Find the best possible option.”**

### Bounded Rationality

> **“Find a satisfactory option within the available time and information.”**

This distinction is extremely important.

---

# Great example: Buying a laptop

Suppose you need a laptop **tomorrow**.

There are 100 different laptops available.

### Rational Model

You might:

This takes a lot of time.

### Bounded Rationality

You have only **2 hours**.

So you say:

> “I need 16 GB RAM, a decent processor, good battery and my budget is ₹70,000.”

You check 5–6 laptops.

You find one that satisfies your requirements.

> “Good enough. I'll take this.”

You **stop searching** even though there may be a slightly better laptop somewhere.

That's **bounded rationality**.

---

# Why is it called "bounded"?

Because rational decision-making is **bounded by limitations**.

---

# Satisficing

This word is very important.

**Satisficing = satisfy + suffice**

It means choosing an option that is **good enough**, rather than trying to find the theoretically perfect option.

### Example

You need a hotel.

You don't compare 500 hotels.

You set:

> Price < ₹3,000  
> Rating > 4.0  
> Near college

The first hotel satisfying these requirements may be selected.

That's **satisficing**.

### Practical in the real world

Because real-world decisions often involve time and information constraints.

Because you stop searching once you find something acceptable.

There may be a **better option**, but you never discover it.

# Recognition-Primed Decision (RPD) Model

This is the **fourth and final decision-making model** in your PDF. It comes after the Rational, Bounded Rationality, and Intuitive models.

## What is RPD?

**Recognition-Primed Decision (RPD)** means making a decision by **recognizing a familiar pattern from previous experience** and then mentally evaluating what is likely to happen.

Your PDF highlights three key ideas:

> **Pattern recognition + expertise + simulation in mind**

### In simple words

> **“I've seen a situation like this before, so I recognize what is happening and know what usually works.”**

But there is one extra step compared with simple intuition:

**You recognize the situation → mentally simulate the possible action → decide.**

---

That's the basic idea of **RPD**.

# 1. Decision Matrix / Weighted Scoring Model

## What is it?

A **Decision Matrix** is a structured method used to compare multiple options using predefined criteria.

Your PDF says that:

- options are compared side-by-side,
- criteria are predefined,
- criteria receive weights according to importance,
- each option is scored,
- scores are multiplied by weights,
- totals help identify the best choice.

### Simple idea

Suppose you have to choose between:

**Goa, Mulshi, Alibag**

You can't simply say:

> “I like Goa.”

You decide using criteria:

- Duration
- Cost
- Fun activities
- Safety

Each criterion gets a **weight** based on importance.

|Cost|4|
|Fun activities|5|
|Safety|4|

So **fun activities** is considered more important than duration.

### 1. Nominal Group Technique (NGT)

NGT is a **structured group brainstorming method** designed to make sure everyone participates and to reduce the influence of dominant personalities.

The process is:

**Step 1 — Silent idea generation**  
Everyone independently writes down ideas.

**Step 2 — Round-robin sharing**  
Each person presents one idea at a time; all ideas are recorded.

**Step 3 — Clarification**  
The group discusses the ideas only to understand/clarify them.

**Step 4 — Individual ranking**  
Each person privately ranks the ideas.

**Step 5 — Group decision**  
The rankings are discussed and the group selects the preferred ideas.

### Example

Your team needs to decide:

> **“Which feature should we add to our college app?”**

Instead of letting the most talkative member dominate:

**Why NGT?**

> **Equal participation + less dominance + structured decision**

# 2. PDSA

**PDSA = Plan → Do → Study → Act**

This is listed in your PDF alongside NGT as a decision-making tool.

Think of it as a **continuous improvement cycle**.

### Plan

Identify the problem and decide what change you want to try.

### Do

Implement the change, usually on a limited/small scale.

### Study

Observe the result and compare it with what you expected.

### Act

Based on the result:

- adopt the change,
- modify it,
- or try something else.

### Example

Your college canteen has very long queues.

**Plan:** Introduce a separate digital-payment counter.

**Do:** Try it for one week.

**Study:** Check waiting time before and after.

**Act:** If it works, continue/expand it; if not, modify the approach.

### Memory

> **PDSA = Try → Observe → Learn → Improve**

# 1. Lewin’s Change Management Model

Lewin's model explains organizational change in **3 simple stages**:

### **Unfreeze → Change → Refreeze**

### 1. Unfreeze

Prepare people for change.

The organization makes employees understand:

- Why the current situation is not sufficient.
    
- Why change is necessary.
    
- What problems will occur if nothing changes.
    

**Example:**  
A company is using an old manual billing system. Management explains that it causes delays and errors and announces that a digital billing system will be introduced.

### 2. Change

Actual transition happens.

People:

- learn new methods,
    
- adopt new technology,
    
- change their behavior,
    
- experiment with the new system.
    

**Example:**  
Employees are trained to use the new billing software.

### 3. Refreeze

The new way becomes the **normal way of working**.

The organization:

- standardizes the new process,
    
- creates policies,
    
- provides rewards/support,
    
- ensures employees continue using it.
    

**Example:**  
The company stops accepting manual billing and makes digital billing the standard process.

### Easy memory

**U-C-R**

> **U = Prepare**  
> **C = Implement**  
> **R = Stabilize**

### Exam answer

> **Lewin's Change Management Model** consists of three stages: **Unfreeze, Change and Refreeze**. Unfreeze prepares employees for change, Change involves implementing the new methods, and Refreeze stabilizes the new practices so that they become the normal way of working.

# 2. Force-Field Analysis

This is associated with **Kurt Lewin**.

The basic idea is:

> **Some forces push for change, while some forces resist change.**

The organization has to examine both and shift the balance toward the desired change.

### Driving Forces

These **support change**.

Examples:

- New technology
    
- Leadership support
    
- Competitive pressure
    
- Customer demand
    

### Restraining Forces

These **oppose change**.

Examples:

- Employee resistance
    
- Lack of training
    
- Fear of job loss
    
- Lack of resources
    

### Example

Suppose a factory wants to introduce automation.

**Driving forces:**

- Faster production
    
- Lower errors
    
- Leadership support
    

**Restraining forces:**

- Employees fear job loss
    
- Employees lack technical skills
    
- High initial cost
    

Management can:

**Increase driving forces**  
→ show benefits, provide management support

**Decrease restraining forces**  
→ train employees, provide job security/redeployment

This shifts the balance toward change.

### Memory trick

> **Force-Field = FOR change vs AGAINST change**

### Exam point

Don't confuse this with a change stage model.

**Lewin 3-step model:** tells **how change happens**.  
**Force-field analysis:** tells **what is helping or resisting change**.

Create a strong group of leaders/champions who support the change.

### 3. Create a Vision for Change

Clearly define:

> "Where are we going?"

Example: "Reduce project delivery time by 25%."

### 4. Communicate the Vision

# 4. ADKAR Model

This one is **very important for fill-in-the-blanks**.

ADKAR was developed by **Jeff Hiatt**, founder of Prosci, in **1996**.

### ADKAR =

> **A — Awareness**  
> **D — Desire**  
> **K — Knowledge**  
> **A — Ability**  
> **R — Reinforcement**

Let's understand the difference carefully.

### A — Awareness

The person understands:

> **WHY is the change necessary?**

Example: Employees understand why the company must move toward EVs.

### D — Desire

The person develops willingness to support the change.

> **Do I WANT to support it?**

### K — Knowledge

The person learns:

> **HOW do I change?**

Example: Engineers receive training in EV technology and battery systems.

### A — Ability

The person can actually **perform the new behavior**.

Knowledge ≠ Ability.

Someone may know how to use software but still be unable to use it properly in real work.

### R — Reinforcement

Ensure the change continues.

Examples:

- rewards,
    
- recognition,
    
- incentives,
    
- celebrating success.
    

### Tata Motors example from your PDF

For Tata Motors' transition toward EVs:

**Awareness:** urgent need for sustainable vehicles  
**Desire:** employees/stakeholders align with the green vision  
**Knowledge:** engineers trained in EV and battery systems  
**Ability:** practical projects such as Nexon EV development  
**Reinforcement:** milestones, incentives and rewards maintain momentum.

### Best memory

> **Know WHY → WANT → KNOW HOW → CAN DO → KEEP DOING**

That's ADKAR.

# 5. Bridges’ Transition Model

This is the one students often confuse with change-management models.

Bridges focuses on the **psychological transition of people**, not simply the organizational change itself.

The PDF gives three stages:

> **Ending/Loss → Neutral Zone → New Beginning**

### 1. Ending / Loss

People first have to let go of the old situation.

They may feel:

- fear,
    
- sadness,
    
- uncertainty,
    
- resistance.
    

Example:

> "We have always used the old system. Why are they replacing it?"

### 2. Neutral Zone

The old way is gone, but the new way is not yet fully comfortable.

This is the **transition period**.

People may experience:

- confusion,
    
- experimentation,
    
- lower productivity,
    
- uncertainty.
    

Example:

Employees are learning the new software but still making mistakes.

### 3. New Beginning

People finally accept and embrace the new way.

They:

- understand the new role,
    
- gain confidence,
    
- adopt new behaviors,
    
- become committed to the new system.

# 3. Kübler-Ross Change Curve

Your PDF lists **Kübler-Ross Change Curve (1969)** immediately after Fisher's model.

For exam purposes, remember that this model describes **emotional reactions to change**.

### 1. Denial

> “This change isn't really necessary.”

The person accepts the reality of the change and starts adapting.

# 1. Definition of Empowerment

### What is empowerment?

**Empowerment = giving employees autonomy, responsibility and decision-making authority.**

In simple words:

### B. Role Ambiguity

The employee is unclear about:

> **"What exactly am I responsible for?"**

Example:

Two employees both believe they have authority to approve a decision.

Result → confusion.

### C. Conflict

When authority and responsibility are distributed among employees, disagreements may arise.

Example:

Employee A wants one implementation.  
Employee B wants another.  
Both have decision-making authority.

→ conflict.

### Memory:

> **ORC = Overload, Role ambiguity, Conflict**

# 2. Empowerment Reduces Resistance

This is a very simple but important relationship in the PDF:

> **Ownership = less fear → smoother transition**

Think about it.

### Without empowerment

Management:

> "We have decided this. You must do it."

Employee:

> "Why should I accept this?"

→ resistance

### With empowerment

Management:

> "We are changing the process. Tell us how it should be implemented."

Employee participates.

Employee thinks:

> "I helped create this."

→ ownership  
→ less fear  
→ greater acceptance

### Flow to memorize

**Empowerment → Participation → Ownership → Less Fear → Less Resistance → Smoother Change**

That's the core idea.
