## 1. What is Conflict?

Your notes define conflict as a **psychological and behavioural reaction** to the perception that another person is:

1. **Keeping you from reaching a goal**
2. **Taking away your right to behave in a particular way**
3. **Violating the expectations of a relationship**

### Easy meaning

Conflict happens when you feel:

> **“This person/group is preventing me from getting what I want or behaving the way I expect.”**

# 2. Types of Group Conflict

There are **3 types** in your notes:

### Individual vs Individual

Two people have a conflict.

**Example:** Two students disagree over who should lead a project.

### Individual vs Team

One person conflicts with the entire team.

**Example:** A team member refuses to follow the group's decision.

### Team vs Team

Two groups/teams conflict.

**Example:** Development team and testing team disagree about a release.

### 🧠 Memorize

**I-I → I-T → T-T**

# 3. Causes of Conflict ⭐⭐⭐

Your notes give several causes.

### 1. Competition for Resources

**More demand + less supply**

Example: Two departments want the same limited budget.

### 2. Task Interdependence

One person's work depends on another person's work.

If one person delays, the other gets affected.

### 3. Jurisdictional Ambiguity

Unclear responsibility about **who has authority over what**.

Example: Two managers both believe they are responsible for approving the same task.

### 4. Communication Barriers

Your notes classify these as:

- **Physical**
- **Cultural**
- **Psychological**

### 5. Beliefs

Certain beliefs can generate conflict:

- Feeling **superior to others**
- Feeling **mistreated**
- Feeling **vulnerable**
- Feeling unable to **trust others**
- Feeling **helpless/powerless**

## 1. Competing / Forcing

### What happens?

You strongly pursue **your own interests**, with little concern for the other person.

**Result:** Win/Lose

> **“MY WAY”**

### Example

Two team members disagree about the project technology.

You say:

> “We're using my approach. That's final.”

### Use when

You need to act decisively or a quick decision is required.

---

# 2. Collaborating ⭐⭐⭐

### What happens?

Both sides openly discuss the problem and try to find a solution that satisfies **both**.

**Result:** Win/Win

> **“OUR WAY”**

### Example

Developer wants faster delivery.

Tester wants more testing.

Instead of choosing one side, they work together and create a testing plan that maintains quality while meeting the deadline.

### Important

This style has:

**High concern for Self + High concern for Others**

---

# 3. Avoiding / Withdrawal

### What happens?

You don't actively pursue your own interests **or** the other person's.

**Result:** Lose/Lose

> **“NO WAY”**

### Example

Two teammates are arguing.

You simply leave the discussion and say:

> “I don't want to get involved.”

The conflict remains unresolved.

---

# 4. Accommodating

### What happens?

You give greater importance to the **other person's needs** than your own.

**Result:** Lose/Win

> **“YOUR WAY”**

### Example

Your teammate wants to use React, but you prefer Vue.

You accept React to maintain harmony in the team.

---

# 5. Compromising

### What happens?

Both sides give up **something** to reach an acceptable solution.

**Result:** Partial Lose/Lose

> **“HALF WAY”**

### Example

One person wants ₹10,000 budget.

Another wants ₹5,000.

They agree on **₹7,500**.

Both get part of what they wanted, but neither gets everything.

# Resolving Conflicts

## 1. Formal Policy at Organizations

Organizations can have **formal policies and procedures** for dealing with conflicts.

Think:

> **Conflict occurs → follow the organization's established procedure**

This gives a structured way to handle disputes.

---

## 2. Conflict Resolution Skills

Your notes emphasize:

- **Cooperation**
- **Compliments**
- **No negative interaction**
- **Mutual similarities**
- **Common goals**

### Goal

> **Reduce tension → Increase trust**

### Easy example

Two team members are arguing.

Instead of attacking each other, they:

- cooperate,
- focus on what they have in common,
- avoid negative interaction,
- identify their common goal.

This reduces tension and improves trust.

---

# 3. Planned Meeting

A planned meeting can be used to deal with the dispute.

Your notes mention two approaches:

### Dispute discussion

The parties discuss the conflict directly.

### Cooperative Problem Solving

Both sides work together to find a solution.

So remember:

> **Planned Meeting → Dispute + Cooperative Problem Solving**

---

# 4. Third-Party Intervention ⭐⭐⭐

When the people involved cannot resolve the conflict themselves, a **third party** can intervene.

There are two important methods:

## Mediation

The third party helps the conflicting parties **reach a mutual agreement**.

The mediator facilitates the discussion but the parties work toward the agreement.

### Memory:

> **MEDIATION → MUTUAL AGREEMENT**


## Arbitration

The third party **chooses/decides the correct side**.

So instead of merely helping the parties reach their own agreement, the arbitrator makes the decision.

### Memory:

> **ARBITRATION → DECISION / CHOOSE THE SIDE**

Your PDF explicitly distinguishes them this way.
