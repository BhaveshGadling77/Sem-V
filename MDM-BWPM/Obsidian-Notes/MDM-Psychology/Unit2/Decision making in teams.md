#  Decision Making in Teams

Now we move to **team decision-making**.

Your PDF says teams can pool **diverse skills and knowledge** and often produce better-quality decisions than individuals, but they require **coordination and open communication**.

## Why use teams?

Imagine you're designing a new bridge.

One person might know:

- structural engineering

Another:

- materials

Another:

- safety

Another:

- cost

When all these people collaborate, the team has access to **multiple perspectives and expertise**.

---

## Advantages

The PDF gives:

### Multiple perspectives

Different people see different aspects of the problem.

### Creativity

More people can produce more ideas.

### Shared responsibility

The decision is shared among team members.

---

## But there are challenges

Teams can also create:

### Groupthink

Everyone starts agreeing instead of critically examining the decision.

### Conflict

Members may disagree about the best option.

### Delays

More people → more discussion → decision can take longer.

---

## Example

Imagine 5 engineers deciding which material to use.

Engineer 1:

> “Steel is strongest.”

Engineer 2:

> “But it's expensive.”

Engineer 3:

> “Composite is lighter.”

Engineer 4:

> “But maintenance could be difficult.”

Engineer 5:

> “Let's consider the environmental impact.”

Now the team has a **much broader view** than one person might have.

But they need proper coordination to convert all these opinions into a decision.

### 1. Nominal Group Technique (NGT)

NGT is a **structured group brainstorming method** designed to make sure everyone participates and to reduce the influence of dominant personalities.

The process is:

**Step 1 — Silent idea generation**  
Everyone independently writes down ideas.

**Step 2 — Round-robin sharing**  
Each person presents one idea at a time; all ideas are recorded.

**Step 3 — Clarification**  
The group discusses the ideas only to understand/clarify them.

**Step 4 — Individual ranking**  
Each person privately ranks the ideas.

**Step 5 — Group decision**  
The rankings are discussed and the group selects the preferred ideas.

### Example

Your team needs to decide:

> **“Which feature should we add to our college app?”**

Instead of letting the most talkative member dominate:

**Why NGT?**

> **Equal participation + less dominance + structured decision**

# 2. PDSA

**PDSA = Plan → Do → Study → Act**

This is listed in your PDF alongside NGT as a decision-making tool.

Think of it as a **continuous improvement cycle**.

### Plan

Identify the problem and decide what change you want to try.

### Do

Implement the change, usually on a limited/small scale.

### Study

Observe the result and compare it with what you expected.

### Act

Based on the result:

- adopt the change,
- modify it,
- or try something else.

### Example

Your college canteen has very long queues.

**Plan:** Introduce a separate digital-payment counter.

**Do:** Try it for one week.

**Study:** Check waiting time before and after.

**Act:** If it works, continue/expand it; if not, modify the approach.

### Memory

> **PDSA = Try → Observe → Learn → Improve**

# 3. Cost-Benefit Analysis

The idea is very straightforward:

> **Compare the expected benefits of an option with its expected costs.**

For example, your company is considering new software.

### Costs

- ₹10 lakh implementation
- training cost
- maintenance

### Benefits

- faster processing
- fewer errors
- reduced labour/time
- increased productivity

You compare the two sides before deciding.

### Simple rule

> **If expected benefits significantly outweigh costs → stronger case for choosing the option.**

For an exam, remember:

**Cost-Benefit Analysis = Costs vs Benefits**

The PDF lists it as one of the decision-making tools.
