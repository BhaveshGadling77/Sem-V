# 4. Six Thinking Hats

This is another decision-making tool listed in your PDF.

The basic idea is:

> **Instead of everyone arguing from their own style, the team deliberately looks at a problem from six different thinking perspectives.**

### The 6 hats

|Hat|Meaning|
|---|---|
|**White**|Facts and information|
|**Red**|Feelings and intuition|
|**Black**|Risks, problems, weaknesses|
|**Yellow**|Benefits, value, positive aspects|
|**Green**|Creativity and alternatives|
|**Blue**|Control, organization, summary|

### Example

Suppose your company wants to launch a new product.

**White:** What data do we have about the market?

**Red:** How do customers/employees feel about it?

**Black:** What could go wrong?

**Yellow:** What benefits could we get?

**Green:** What new ideas or alternatives exist?

**Blue:** How do we organize the discussion and decide?

So instead of:

> “I think it's good!”

the team systematically asks:

> **Facts? Feelings? Risks? Benefits? Creativity? Process?**

### Memory trick

> **White = Facts**  
> **Red = Feelings**  
> **Black = Risks**  
> **Yellow = Benefits**  
> **Green = Creativity**  
> **Blue = Control**

This is particularly easy to get as a **match-the-pairs** question.
