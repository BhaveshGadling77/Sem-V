# 1. Social Exchange Theory — Homans (1961)

**George Homans (1961)** with Social Exchange Theory.
### Core idea

Think of group membership as an **exchange**.

A person tends to continue participating in a group when the **rewards they receive** make the relationship worthwhile compared with its costs.

### Simple example

You join a study group.

**Rewards:**

- Get help with difficult topics
- Share notes
- Get support

**Costs:**

- Time spent in meetings
- Effort helping others
- Conflicts/disagreements

If the benefits are worthwhile, you're more likely to remain an active member.

### 🧠 Remember

**What does it cost me if I DO help?**

The matrix gives four situations:

| Benefits of NOT helping / Cost of helping | Benefit of helping / Cost of NOT helping: **High**    | **Low**              |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| **High**                                  | **Indirect intervention or reappraise the situation** | **Not help**         |
| **Low**                                   | **Directly help**                                     | **Depends on norms** |

This is the **exact structure shown in your slide**.

# Understand each box

### 1. High / High → Indirect intervention or reappraise the situation

If:

- Benefit of helping is **high**
- But benefit of NOT helping / cost of helping is also **high**

Then the person may **intervene indirectly** or **rethink/reappraise the situation**.

👉 **Memory:**  
**High + High → Indirect / Reappraise**

### 2. High benefit of helping / Low benefit of NOT helping → Directly help

Here:

- Helping has a **high benefit**
- Not helping has a **low benefit/cost implication**

So the person is likely to **directly help**.

👉 **Memory:**

**High benefit of helping + Low benefit of NOT helping → DIRECT HELP**

---

### 3. Low benefit of helping / High benefit of NOT helping → Not help

Here:

- Helping provides little benefit
- Not helping provides greater benefit / avoids greater cost

Therefore:

**NOT HELP**

👉 Very easy:

**Helping = low benefit → Don't help**

---

### 4. Low / Low → Depends on norms

Both sides are low.

So there isn't a strong economic/reward-based reason either way.

The decision therefore **depends on social norms**.

👉 **Memory:**

**Low + Low → NORMS****

**Norms = what people in that society, group, or situation generally consider appropriate behavior.***

## 2. Social Identity Theory — Tajfel & Turner (1979) ⭐⭐⭐

### Core idea

People derive part of their identity from the **groups they belong to**.

For example:

> "I am a COEP student."

Being a member of that group becomes part of how you see yourself.

### Simple example

Suppose there are two project teams:

**Team A** vs **Team B**

A member of Team A may:

- Feel connected to Team A
- Identify themselves as a Team A member
- View Team A more positively
- Feel that Team A's success is personally important

This is **social identity**.

### 🧠 Remember

**Social Identity Theory → "Who am I?" through my group membership**

---
# ⭐ Difference — Very Important for Match the Pairs

|Social Exchange Theory|Social Identity Theory|
|---|---|
|**Homans (1961)**|**Tajfel & Turner (1979)**|
|Focuses on **exchange**|Focuses on **identity**|
|Rewards and costs|Group membership and identity|
|"What do I get from this group?"|"Who am I as a member of this group?"|
